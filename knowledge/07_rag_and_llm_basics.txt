# RAG and Local LLM Basics

RAG means Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Instead of hoping the model remembers facts, you search your own documents first and give the best passages to the model together with the question.

Pipeline: load documents, split into chunks (500-800 characters), convert chunks to embeddings (vectors), store them, embed the user question, find the most similar chunks with cosine similarity, put them into the prompt, let the LLM answer.

Why RAG helps small models: a 0.5B model has little memory of facts, so good context matters more than model size. Clear, focused knowledge files give better answers than long messy ones.

Tips for better answers: write knowledge files as short titled sections, use simple sentences, include examples, avoid duplicates, and add new files for every topic students ask about.

Prompt design: give the model a role, rules, the context, and the question. Ask for structure (headings, bullets). Low temperature (0.2-0.5) gives stable answers.

GGUF and llama.cpp: GGUF is a quantised model format. Q4_K_M keeps quality while cutting size, so Qwen2.5 0.5B needs only about 500 MB. llama-cpp-python loads it and runs on CPU.

Limitations to mention in viva: small models can hallucinate, they are weak at long reasoning, and answers depend on retrieval quality.
